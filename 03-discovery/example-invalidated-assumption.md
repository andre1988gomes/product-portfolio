# Example — Invalidating a Core Assumption Through Discovery

---

## Assumption Tested

Users perceive a specific workflow as a primary source of friction and would adopt a new feature to reduce it.

---

## Why This Assumption Mattered

The initiative was already on the roadmap and positioned as a value driver.

If the assumption was wrong:
- adoption would be low
- delivered value would be marginal
- effort would be wasted on the wrong problem

---

## Discovery Signals Collected

- qualitative user interviews focused on recent usage
- behavioral data around task completion and drop-off
- full workflow mapping to identify real pain points

---

## What We Found

- users did not perceive the targeted workflow as a primary problem
- friction existed, but was not decisive
- an adjacent problem was consistently described as more painful and time-consuming
- users explicitly stated they would not change behavior for the proposed solution

---

## Decision

The original direction was invalidated.

The initiative was pivoted toward the adjacent, validated problem.

Discovery was treated as a **success**, even though it invalidated prior assumptions.

---

## Key Learning

Repeated signals are not validation.

Discovery must be allowed to challenge direction, not just refine scope.
