# Product Requirements

This folder contains examples of **outcome-driven product requirements**.

The goal of these documents is not to specify solutions in detail, but to:
- clarify the problem being solved
- define success in terms of outcomes
- make constraints and non-goals explicit
- guide delivery decisions without over-prescribing implementation

These PRDs are intentionally lightweight and decision-oriented.

---

## How I Use Product Requirements

A product requirement document exists to answer four questions:

1. **Why does this matter?**  
2. **What outcome are we trying to achieve?**  
3. **What constraints shape the solution space?**  
4. **What are we explicitly not trying to solve?**

If a document does not help answer these questions, it is unnecessary.

---

## What These PRDs Are Not

These documents are **not**:
- detailed functional specifications
- contracts between product and engineering
- exhaustive lists of requirements
- substitutes for discovery or collaboration

Details emerge during delivery.  
Decisions and intent must be clear upfront.

---

## Structure Used

All PRDs in this folder follow the same structure:

- Context & Problem Statement  
- Desired Outcomes  
- Constraints & Assumptions  
- Non-Goals  
- Success Signals  

This structure is designed to preserve **intent and reasoning**, even as solutions evolve.
