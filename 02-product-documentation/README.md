# Product Documentation

This section contains examples of product documentation used to **translate intent into execution** without losing clarity, ownership, or outcome focus.

The goal of this documentation is not completeness or process compliance.  
It exists to support **decision-making, alignment, and delivery under constraints**.

---

## What This Documentation Is

The artefacts in this section demonstrate how I use documentation to:

- clarify product intent and outcomes
- align stakeholders with different incentives
- make trade-offs explicit
- reduce ambiguity for delivery teams
- preserve decision context over time

Documentation is treated as a **tool**, not a deliverable.

---

## What This Documentation Is Not

These artefacts are **not**:
- exhaustive specifications
- contract-style requirement documents
- documentation written for its own sake
- substitutes for collaboration

If a document does not enable a decision or reduce risk, it should not exist.

---

## Types of Documentation in This Section

### Product Requirements (Outcome-Driven)

Examples of product requirements that focus on:
- outcomes rather than features
- constraints rather than wish lists
- success criteria instead of scope accumulation

These documents are intentionally concise and decision-oriented.

---

### Decision Records

Lightweight records capturing:
- what decision was made
- which options were considered
- which trade-offs were accepted
- why a particular path was chosen

The goal is to preserve **reasoning**, not to justify retrospectively.

---

### Backlog and Execution Artefacts

Examples of how product intent is translated into:
- user stories
- slices of value
- backlog structures that reflect risk and learning, not just delivery order

These artefacts aim to keep teams outcome-focused during execution.

---

### Alignment and Communication Documents

Short-form documents used to:
- align stakeholders
- communicate trade-offs
- explain unpopular or counterintuitive decisions
- reduce misalignment before it turns into delivery friction

---

## How to Read This Section

These artefacts are best read alongside the **Case Studies**.

Each document reflects:
- a specific context
- a specific decision or risk
- a moment in time

They are not universal templates, but **examples of applied product thinking**.

---

## Why This Section Exists

Strong product work depends on:
- clarity of intent
- shared understanding of constraints
- explicit trade-offs
- preserved context over time

This section shows how documentation can enable those outcomes without becoming bureaucratic.
