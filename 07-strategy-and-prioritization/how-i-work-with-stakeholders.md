# How I Work With Stakeholders

## Evidence Metadata

| Field | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Used in | Entire portfolio |
| Decision supported | Make leadership, alignment, and influence visible as part of senior product work. |
| Evidence type | Qualitative, Leadership |
| Confidence level | High |
| Outcome influenced | Better alignment, clearer trade-offs, and faster movement once a decision is made. |

## Working Style

I work best when product leadership is both clear and collaborative:

- clear about the outcome we are trying to create
- explicit about the trade-offs we are accepting
- collaborative about how we get there

I do not treat alignment as agreement for its own sake. I treat it as making the decision logic visible enough that people can challenge it early and execute it confidently once it is made.

## With Leadership

I try to give leaders three things:

- a crisp statement of the decision
- the trade-off being accepted
- the risk of delaying or avoiding the decision

This is especially important when the right product call is uncomfortable because it changes direction, slows visible delivery, or rejects a locally attractive shortcut.

## With Engineering

I work with engineering as partners in problem framing, not just delivery recipients.

That usually means:

- bringing constraints into the product conversation early
- separating hard constraints from assumed constraints
- avoiding documents that prescribe implementation before the product trade-off is understood

In the strongest examples in this portfolio, engineering context improved the product decision rather than merely limiting it.

## With Design And Research

I value design and research most when they sharpen the decision:

- what user behavior matters
- what pain is primary versus secondary
- what solution assumptions are unsafe

I prefer focused learning loops to broad artifact production.

## With Business And Functional Stakeholders

I try to reduce misalignment by making non-goals and accepted costs explicit.

That often means saying things like:

- what this initiative will not solve
- what short-term cost we are accepting
- what signal would cause us to revisit the decision

This usually creates better alignment than trying to maintain optimism through ambiguity.

## What Stakeholder Leadership Looks Like In Practice

Across the case studies in this repo, stakeholder leadership showed up as:

- pushing to validate a problem before protecting a plan
- defending correctness over convenience when optimization created hidden risk
- clarifying ownership when accountability for the outcome was unclear
- using concise documentation to make disagreement productive rather than personal
