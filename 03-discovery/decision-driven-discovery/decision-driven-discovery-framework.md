# Decision-Driven Discovery Framework

**Purpose:**  
Determine when discovery is sufficient to make a product decision — and when continuing discovery adds diminishing or negative value.

---

## Core Principle

**Discovery is complete when the decision risk is lower than the delivery risk.**

Continuing discovery beyond that point delays value and erodes accountability.

---

## Step 1 — Define the Decision Explicitly

Before starting discovery, answer:

> What concrete decision will this discovery enable?

Examples:
- proceed or stop an initiative
- pivot problem framing
- change target user or outcome
- deprioritize from the roadmap

If no clear decision exists, discovery should not start.

---

## Step 2 — Identify Decision-Critical Unknowns

List unknowns that materially affect the decision.

Focus on:
- user behavior uncertainty
- value uncertainty
- feasibility uncertainty
- ecosystem or dependency uncertainty

Ignore unknowns that do not change the decision.

---

## Step 3 — Set a Discovery Exit Criterion

Define **what evidence is sufficient** to decide.

Examples:
- qualitative consistency across interviews
- clear invalidation of a core assumption
- convergence of behavioral signals
- confirmation that risk cannot be reduced further without delivery

This criterion should be defined *before* evidence is collected.

---

## Step 4 — Run Targeted Discovery

Execute only activities that reduce decision risk.

Rules:
- prefer disconfirming evidence
- stop once signals converge
- avoid expanding scope mid-discovery

Discovery velocity matters less than signal quality.

---

## Step 5 — Evaluate Signal Convergence

Ask:
- Are signals consistent or contradictory?
- Do remaining unknowns still affect the decision?
- Is additional discovery likely to change the outcome?

If signals converge, discovery should stop.

---

## Step 6 — Decide and Commit

Explicitly choose one:
- proceed
- pivot
- stop

Document:
- the decision
- the evidence used
- the risks consciously accepted

Ownership begins at this point.

---

## Common Failure Modes

- Continuing discovery because stakeholders are uncomfortable deciding.
- Treating discovery as progress without decisions.
- Reopening discovery after a decision without new evidence.
- Expanding discovery scope instead of reducing uncertainty.

---

## Signals Discovery Should Stop

- Evidence consistently points in one direction.
- Remaining unknowns are delivery risks, not decision risks.
- Additional discovery repeats known insights.
- The cost of delay exceeds the cost of being wrong.

---

## Relationship to Other Frameworks

This framework complements:
- **Assumption Mapping** (what to test)
- **Outcome Over Optimization** (how to decide under variability)

Together, they form a coherent discovery-to-decision system.
