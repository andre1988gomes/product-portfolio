## Evidence Ladder

When documenting results in this portfolio, I use the strongest evidence that is safe and defensible:

1. Exact metric when disclosure is safe.
2. Anonymized range when exact disclosure is unsafe.
3. Directional change plus scale context when exact numbers are not appropriate.
4. Qualitative or operational signal when it better represents reality than a weak metric would.

## What I Consider A Good Success Measure

A useful success measure should do at least one of the following:

- show customer value changed
- show business or operational performance changed
- reduce uncertainty around an important product decision
- reveal whether a trade-off actually worked

## Example Signal Types

| Signal type | Example use |
| --- | --- |
| Quantitative | Adoption, conversion, failure rate, reduction in incident frequency, cycle-time improvement |
| Operational | Renegotiation frequency, deviation categories, response latency, reconfiguration speed |
| Proxy | Expected adoption based on repeated user signals, reduced exposure to silent-risk paths |
| Qualitative | User willingness to change behavior, stakeholder trust, partner feedback on explainability |

## What I Avoid

- vanity metrics detached from a decision
- exact numbers that cannot be safely disclosed
- inflated impact claims without evidence
- success language that hides the cost of the trade-off

## Practical Rule

If the strongest safe evidence is qualitative, I label it qualitative.

If the evidence is operational or a proxy, I say so directly.

This keeps the portfolio credible and makes the trade-offs behind each result easier to trust.
