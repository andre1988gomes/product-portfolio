# Case Studies

This section contains real-world product case studies focused on **decision-making under uncertainty**, not feature delivery or process compliance.

Each case study documents a concrete product situation where assumptions were challenged, trade-offs were made explicit, and decisions were taken under real constraints.

These are not success stories by default.  
They are **judgment stories**.

---

## What These Case Studies Are

Each case study aims to show:

- how product problems were framed (and sometimes reframed)
- which constraints shaped the decision space
- which alternatives were seriously considered
- why certain options were explicitly rejected
- how trade-offs were made under pressure
- what outcomes were achieved
- what I would do differently with the same information today

The focus is on **product thinking**, not on polished execution narratives.

---

## What These Case Studies Are Not

These case studies are **not**:
- feature descriptions
- delivery reports
- process walkthroughs
- post-rationalized success stories
- generic “lessons learned”

Where discovery invalidated direction, that is treated as a valid and valuable outcome.

---

## How to Read These Case Studies

Each case follows a consistent structure:

1. **Context**  
   Why the initiative existed and why it mattered.

2. **Constraints**  
   The realities that limited what was possible or safe.

3. **Options Considered**  
   Viable alternatives and why they were rejected.

4. **Decision**  
   The explicit product decision and its trade-offs.

5. **Outcome**  
   What changed as a result, including costs and avoided risks.

6. **What I’d Do Differently**  
   Reflection focused on decision quality and learning.

Not all cases include metrics.  
When quantitative data is unavailable or misleading, qualitative evidence is used instead and made explicit.

---

## Why These Case Studies Exist

The purpose of this collection is to demonstrate how product decisions are made when:
- information is incomplete
- constraints conflict
- delivery pressure is real
- and trade-offs cannot be avoided

Product work is not about always being right.  
It is about **making the best possible decision with the information available at the time**.
