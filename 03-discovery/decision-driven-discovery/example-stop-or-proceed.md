# Example — Stopping Discovery and Committing to a Pivot

---

## Decision to Be Made

Should the initiative proceed with the originally scoped solution, or should the problem framing be changed?

---

## Decision-Critical Unknowns

- Do users experience the targeted workflow as a primary pain point?
- Would users change behavior if the proposed solution existed?
- Is the perceived friction strong enough to justify investment?

---

## Exit Criterion Defined Upfront

Discovery would stop if:
- users consistently described a different problem as more painful
- users explicitly stated they would not adopt the proposed solution
- signals converged across multiple sources

---

## Discovery Signals Collected

- qualitative interviews across active users
- behavioral data review
- workflow mapping

---

## Signal Convergence

Signals were consistent:
- the targeted workflow was not a primary pain point
- an adjacent problem dominated user frustration
- adoption likelihood for the proposed solution was low

Additional discovery was unlikely to change the outcome.

---

## Decision

Discovery stopped.

The initiative pivoted to the adjacent validated problem.

This decision accepted:
- short-term delivery disruption
- stakeholder discomfort

In exchange for:
- higher likelihood of meaningful impact
- reduced risk of shipping low-value functionality

---

## Key Learning

Discovery discipline is not about learning more —  
it is about knowing **when learning is sufficient to decide**.
